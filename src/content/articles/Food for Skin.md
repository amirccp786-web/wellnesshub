Skin is the body’s largest organ, and it serves us throughout our lives. Proper skincare not only helps us look youthful and fresh, but it also supports overall health. 

Skin acts as a protective barrier between our body and the living environment. Consequently, it is susceptible to damage from various factors.

## **Skin Health and Food** 

According to Jean Krutmann, a professor of dermatology at the Leibniz Research Institute for Environmental Medicine, "Air pollution and changing weather conditions also play a role in shaping the structure of the skin." 

He says, "For decades, we believed that ultraviolet (UV) rays from the sun were the primary cause of changes in the skin's appearance and the aging process. However, we now know this is not the case." 

Nevertheless, Professor Krutmann explains that other rays in sunlight—including infrared rays—can also negatively affect the skin. 

He further states, "An increase in temperature also has a detrimental effect on the skin." 

Aging is another significant factor contributing to changes in the skin. As a person ages, the protein levels of “collagen”—which provides structure and strength to the skin, while helping to prevent wrinkles and sagging—gradually begin to decline. 

However, one crucial—and frequently overlooked—element of healthy, radiant skin is our diet. 

From nutrient-rich healthy fats to gut-friendly fermented foods, what you eat directly impacts your skin's vitality. If you want to keep your complexion fresh, vibrant, and youthful for years to come, understanding the powerful role of diet is essential. 

### **1. Colorful Fruits and Vegetables** 

Numerous studies indicate that a diet rich in plant-based foods plays a significant role in maintaining healthy skin. 

Try to eat a range of vibrant fruits and vegetables because they are rich in antioxidants that help shield the skin from sunshine and pollution. 

Carotenoids, such as lutein, lycopene, and beta-carotene, are abundant in bell peppers, tomatoes, and carrots. 

According to Volker Bohm, a professor of nutrition at the Friedrich Schiller University of Jena in Germany, "These compounds accumulate in the body's fatty tissues and help protect the skin against ultraviolet (UV) rays." 

He notes, "While the use of sunscreen remains important, people typically apply it only when they expect to spend an extended period outdoors in the sun, rather than daily. In contrast, a diet rich in carotenoids provides the skin with a foundational level of protection against UV rays throughout the year." 

Pairing foods like tomatoes or carrots with a small amount of healthy fat—such as olive oil—makes it easier to absorb the antioxidants they contain. 

In the body, beta-carotene can also be converted into vitamin A. Both the proliferation of skin cells and the defense of the skin against infections depend on this vitamin. 

Furthermore, carotenoids stimulate skin cells to produce hyaluronic acid. This substance plays a key role in maintaining the skin's firmness and elasticity. Because of these hydrating and firming properties, hyaluronic acid is a popular ingredient in many moisturizers and anti-aging creams. 

These pigmented compounds can also boost collagen levels. A small-scale study showed higher levels of collagen and elastin in the skin of women who took daily carotenoid supplements for ten months. This implies that these substances might aid in preventing collagen from deteriorating. 

Furthermore, eating a vibrant variety of colorful fruits and vegetables can enhance skin tone and overall appearance, creating a healthy glow. However, it is essential to eat these foods consistently to reap these benefits. 

Professor Jean Krutmann states that "It generally takes eight to ten weeks for any antioxidant to provide measurable protection against the harmful effects of the environment." 

Fruits such as mangoes have been found to help the skin retain a youthful appearance, even as one ages. 

A University of California, Davis study led by researcher Vivian Pham examined postmenopausal women who ate 85 grams of mango four times weekly for 16 weeks. The researchers found that this regimen reduced the severity of existing wrinkles and slowed the formation of new ones. 

While further research is needed, experts suggest these benefits may stem not only from mangoes’ carotenoids, but also from other antioxidants like flavonoids and mangiferin. Interestingly, the study also found that eating 250 grams of mangoes daily actually led to an increase in wrinkles. 

Robert Heckman, co-author of the study and nutrition professor at the University of California, Davis, notes that while the exact reason remains unclear, high natural sugar content in mangoes may be a contributing factor. 

According to Heckman, "Although this study was conducted on a limited scale, a subsequent study yielded similar results. However, the findings of this second study have not yet been published." 

Vitamin C is also abundant in citrus fruits and mangoes. This vitamin functions as an antioxidant and aids in the formation of collagen, which can help shield the skin from damage caused by the sun. 

Research suggests that Vitamin C may also help in maintaining skin moisture and reducing uneven skin tone. Professor Jean Krutmann says, "This is why Vitamin C is widely used in skincare products and dietary supplements."

### **2. Healthy Fats** 

Increasing your intake of nuts and seeds may help your skin. 

Participants in a small-scale study were split into two groups. One group consumed almonds as a snack for 16 weeks, while the other group consumed a different snack. The results indicated that those who ate almonds had fewer visible skin wrinkles. 

Vitamin E, which can help shield skin cells from harm and the effects of aging, is also abundant in nuts and seeds. A deficiency in this vitamin can negatively impact skin health and slow down the wound-healing process. Almonds, hazelnuts, pine nuts, and various seed oils are excellent sources of Vitamin E. Nuts and seeds also contain omega-6 fatty acids, which can help strengthen the skin's protective barrier and retain moisture. 

Another type of healthy fat is omega-3, which is found in fish. 

Fish rich in omega-3 oil include salmon, sardines, and mackerel. Eating these fish may lessen the skin's susceptibility to sunlight, protecting it from sun damage, according to research. 

Fatty fish can also help preserve the collagen already present in the skin and reduce inflammation. Collagen is a protein that provides the skin with strength and structure.

### **3. Foods that Improve Gut Health** 

We consume a diverse diet rich in fresh fruits, vegetables, nuts, and seeds. It not only benefits the beneficial microbes in our gut but can also improve skin health. 

There is a strong connection between gut health and the skin. Researchers refer to this relationship as the "gut-skin axis." 

According to Professor of Nutrition Robert Hackman, "When we eat a variety of plant-based foods, the microbes in the gut release compounds that possess anti-inflammatory properties and help protect cells from damage. These compounds also affect the skin." 

Maintaining a healthy ecosystem of beneficial gut microbes can also strengthen the immune system, which, in turn, may help alleviate certain skin issues. Consuming a variety of plant-based foods is beneficial. These include fruits, vegetables, lentils, beans, whole grains, herbs, and spices. 

Such a diet can also help reduce stress, improve sleep, and protect against certain skin conditions. These conditions include psoriasis, which can cause dry, scaly, itchy, and painful patches to form on the skin. 

Fermented foods are also beneficial for promoting the growth of healthy bacteria in the gut. Examples include sauerkraut (fermented cabbage), kimchi, kombucha, and yogurt.

While you may not always look 30 years old, choosing a healthy diet can improve the chances of keeping your skin healthy for years to come. Additionally, sunscreen use is also important.
